# An explicit observation that distinguishes the ladder closures

Consider the straight and twisted cubic ladders of even width k>=8 from [the finite-horizon note](horizon.md). Time is measured from the specified reachable state. Loss means the decrease in the number Q of legal pairs. Put h=(k-4)/2.

**Working proposition.** The observable loss word (7,8,...,6+h) has positive probability in precisely one closure: the straight closure for odd h, and the twisted closure for even h. It is realized there by exactly 2k h! ordered, unoriented-edge histories. For every 1<=t<h, the two closures have equal numbers of histories with the loss word (7,8,...,6+t). This last equality concerns just that word, not the entire joint law. In particular the finite-horizon separation from the earlier note cannot persist indefinitely even for the scalar observable Q alone.

### Why the history becomes a packing problem

In **any** d-regular triangle-free initial graph, at step j (beginning with j=1) the loss on adding uv is at most 1+2d+(j-1). In addition to the chosen pair only pairs from u to current neighbors of v or vice versa can close. Each previously added edge contributes at most one to the endpoint degree sum, because uv has not yet been added. Equality forces every preceding edge to touch u or v, and every candidate additional loss to have been open immediately beforehand. A history achieving equality at every step therefore has pairwise intersecting added edges. Two distinct such edges have a unique common endpoint; every further edge either contains that endpoint or completes a triangle with the first two, which is forbidden. Thus for t>=2 a unique center x is forced.

For a fixed center, such histories are exactly the orderings of sets Y of t leaves satisfying:
1. every y in Y has initial distance at least four from x (infinite distance allowed);
2. distinct leaves have initial distance at least three.

For necessity, an initial path of length one or two prevents the new spoke from being legal; a path of length three means a candidate pair to a neighbor of the opposite endpoint was already closed. Earlier additions cannot reopen it. On adding the later of two spokes, the pair between their leaves must be open, ruling out initial distance at most two. For sufficiency, just before adding xy the new neighbors of x are the earlier leaves; y still has exactly its initial neighbors. The distance conditions ensure the spoke is legal. Every old neighbor of x is open to y by condition 1, and every earlier leaf is open to y by condition 2 (the new edges all contain x and xy is not present). Conversely each neighbor of y is open to x: a previously added spoke could only spoil this if an earlier leaf were that neighbor or adjacent to it, forbidden by condition 2. All candidate losses occur. This argument is independent of leaf order. For a single spoke count oriented centers and divide by two.

### Counting the boundary effect

Root x in column zero. Unroll the strip along columns 0,...,k. At columns 0,1,2 and k-2,k-1 neither row is eligible; at columns 3 and k-3 only the row opposite the nearest lifted copy of x is eligible; the intervening columns allow either row. This follows by taking at most three rail/rung steps from x, including the seam. The permitted strip is therefore columns 3 through k-3 inclusive. Its two prescribed endpoint rows agree in the straight closure and differ in the twisted closure. The seam cannot shorten a path between eligible leaves to length two: the other way round between the two endpoint columns alone requires six rail steps. For k=8 the eligible strip has three columns; the description still applies.

A leaf packing uses at most one row per column, never uses adjacent columns, and if successive selected columns are two apart their rows must be opposite. At spacing three or greater the row choices are independent. Fix the set of selected columns. Row constraints split into chains with successive spacing two. Changing the prescribed row at the right endpoint does not change the count unless **both prescribed ends lie in the same chain**: if the right is present but separate, flip every row in its chain. Both ends lie in one chain precisely for the column set 3,5,...,k-3. No additional column fits. This is the unique set of size h bridging the two boundaries; in fact h is the maximum packing size, and this is the unique maximum column set because the interval length is 2h-1. Its rows are forced to alternate, so the endpoint prescriptions match precisely in the claimed closure. Per center this gives one admissible set there and none in the other. Unique centers and the h! orderings give the stated count.

The initial Q is common to both closures. For each history of this word, its probability is the reciprocal of the product of its pre-move Q values. No distributional independence is used. The first few nonzero counts are 32 (k=8), 120 (k=10), and 576 (k=12). This gives an explicit support difference by h moves, while the proved general agreement for k>6t gives a lower bound on when *any* discrepancy can appear. Closing that gap requires controlling words other than the maximum-loss word.

**Status.** This is an elementary proof for this construction, not a literature priority claim. For context, the process and its open/partial pair variables are classical; see [Bohman](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~tbohman/papers/r3t.pdf). The reduction to leaf sets is a distance-packing count (independent sets in a graph square after removing a radius-three ball), not a new definition of packing. This is a rare-event statement about reachable starting graphs, not typical play from the empty graph.
