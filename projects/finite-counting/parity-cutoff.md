# Adding the parity of the conflict count

In the [two-observation experiment](calibrated-moments.md), suppose that along with low binomial moments we record the parity law of the conflict count in each experiment. For a simple original host with at least three paths and total length m>=6, the sharp common moment order is **floor(m/2)+1**, instead of the moment-only bound m-2 established for m>=12. These are exact population statistics.

**Parity.** Write each conflict bit modulo two as 1 plus the twist bit plus both endpoint color bits. On summation, internal vertices cancel. The terminal bits survive exactly when the number r of paths is odd. Thus for even r the count has constant parity m plus the total twist modulo two; for odd r it is parity-uniform. Adding a positive length-two path changes r by one and m by two. Exactly one parity law is constant; the two parity observations therefore determine r modulo two and the product sigma of all original signs. In particular the two candidates in each of the earlier seven near-ties give *disjoint* probed count supports despite agreeing in all moments through m-3.

**Upper bound.** Set h=floor(m/2). Moments through h+1 recover E through h+1 and O through h-1 by the [calibrated transform](calibrated-paths.md). When r is odd, complementing a subset gives E_j=sigma O_(m-j). These two observed ranges cover E at every degree, for both m=2h and m=2h+1. Hence O too is known.

When r is even each polynomial separately satisfies the same reciprocity (its degree-j and degree-(m-j) coefficients differ by the factor sigma). E is fully known. Two possible O polynomials can differ only by a multiple of x^h for m=2h, or of x^h(1+sigma x) for m=2h+1. Choose t=1 in the first case and t=sigma in the second; either difference, if nonzero, is nonzero at t.

In fact O(t) is determined. The quantities E(t)+O(t) and E(t)-O(t) are the products of (1+s_i t^l_i) and (1-s_i t^l_i), respectively, hence nonnegative. If E(t)=0 both are zero. Otherwise all effective signs s_i t^l_i coincide, and O(t) is E(t) times that common sign. To learn the sign, inspect O(tx): its first nonzero coefficient is the signed multiplicity at the shortest length. Under uniform effective signs no cancellation occurs there. This coefficient is visible through h-1 because even r>=4 implies the shortest length is at most floor(m/4). Thus O(t) is fixed, eliminating the last coefficient ambiguity. With E and O known the existing factor decoder gives the signed type.

**Lower bound.** For m=2h+1 use lengths (1,h,h), and for m=2h use lengths (2,h-1,h-1). Compare all positive signs to flipping both of the equal designated long paths. This preserves r and sigma and hence both parity observations. With lengths written (a,b,b), the difference of the original even polynomials is 4x^(a+b) and that of the odd polynomials is 4x^b. The difference after probing is their sum with the latter multiplied by x^2. In both parameter choices the first distinguishing order across the two experiments is min(a+b,b+2)=h+1. Both hosts are simple, including m=6.

[Finite check](parity_cutoff.py): for each m from 6 through 25 enumerate all signed types, group by path-count parity and sign product, and compare exact moment prefixes. The minimum common cutoff is floor(m/2)+1 at each of the twenty totals. The proof does not presume availability of individual edge observations or terminal colors.


**Sampling limitation.** The last pair also shows why an exact-law inverse result need not be a practical statistical estimator. Write TV for total variation distance. The original pair has TV at most 2 times 2^(-b); the probed pair has TV at most 4 times 2^(-b). In particular even observing each entire conflict count, rather than selected moments, can require exponentially many independent samples in this model.

Indeed a transformed difference term 4x^j on a host of N edges contributes 4 times 2^(-N) times (z-1)^j (z+1)^(N-j) to the probability generating function. Whenever b<=j<=N-b, take out (z^2-1)^b. The sum of absolute coefficients of the resulting product is at most 2^b times 2^(N-2b), by submultiplicativity. Thus the term contributes at most 4 times 2^(-b) to the sum of absolute probability differences. There is one term for the original and two for the probed pair (counted with multiplicity); all satisfy the indicated bounds. Divide by two for TV. As a check, for a=1 the original difference is proportional to (z^2-1)^b(z-1); the even and odd powers do not overlap and the stated original bound is attained.

With fresh independent colorings, an observer choosing either host adaptively for each draw gets transcript TV at most n times 4 times 2^(-b) after n samples. Couple the draws while the two transcripts agree: then the observer chooses the same experiment in both, and the conditional probability of first disagreement is at most the one-draw TV bound. Summing over the draws proves the assertion, including for randomized choices with shared observer randomness. Success probability at least 2/3 on each of the two inputs requires transcript TV at least 1/3. Hence n>=2^b/12 is necessary. This lower bound concerns observing only counts on these two hosts; labeled edge conflicts or arbitrary alternative interventions are different data.
