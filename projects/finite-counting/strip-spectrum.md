# Recovering a seam permutation from closed walks

Take k>=3 columns of q>=2 vertices each. Make each column a clique. Between consecutive columns join like labels; on the last-to-first seam use a permutation of the q labels. The two cubic ladders in the [triangle-free process example](maximal-loss.md) are the q=2 case. This section studies the initial graph's adjacency spectrum. For larger fibers the columns themselves can contain triangles.

**Proposition.** Within this class the adjacency spectrum determines k, q and the permutation's cycle type, and hence the graph up to isomorphism. For fixed k,q, counts of closed walks of lengths at most **k floor(q/2)** suffice for recovery. This cutoff is best possible. For q=2 the first different closed-walk count of the straight and twisted ladders is at length k, with difference 4k.

**Decomposition.** Let R be the adjacency matrix of the rail edges alone and A that of the full graph. The subspace W of vectors constant on each column is invariant under both; its orthogonal complement is invariant by symmetry. On W, R acts as the cycle adjacency C and A acts as C+(q−1)I. On the complement the within-column clique adjacency acts as −I, so A=R−I. Thus for every m the traces of A^j through j=m determine the traces of R^j through j=m: subtract the known contribution from W, apply the binomial expansion to R=A+I on the complement, then add back the known rail contribution on W. The transformation is triangular with leading coefficient one. If k,q are initially unknown, the spectrum gives the vertex count n and the average degree tr(A²)/n=q+1, hence q and k=n/q.

**Earliest distinction.** Each seam cycle of length l gives one rail cycle on kl vertices. For a cycle of length L>=3, a closed m-step walk rooted at one vertex is a sequence of +1 and −1 steps whose sum is divisible by L. For m<L only zero displacement is possible; at m=L exactly two extra walks are possible. Compare two different seam types and take l to be the least length whose multiplicities c_l and c'_l differ. Remove equal shorter rail cycles on both sides. The remaining graphs have equal total vertex counts, so their zero-displacement contributions cancel at all lengths. The first unequal trace for R is therefore at m=kl, with difference **2kl(c_l−c'_l)**. The triangular transformation shows that A has exactly the same first difference. This recovers the type from traces by distinguishing every pair of types. Equal types produce isomorphic graphs by permuting the labels identically in all columns.

After canceling common parts of two distinct partitions of q, at least one residual partition has at least two parts; otherwise both would have a single part of equal size. Both of those parts are at least l. Therefore l<=floor(q/2). The one-part partition (q) and the two-part partition (floor(q/2),ceil(q/2)) attain equality. In particular short walk counts can agree even for connected regular graphs in this restricted family.

**Sources and verification.** The graph is a Cartesian graph bundle over a cycle. [General work on Cartesian bundles](https://users.fmf.uni-lj.si/mohar/Reprints/1995/BM95_JGT19_Klavzar_GraphBundles.pdf) and [Dalfó et al. on spectra of lifts](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10776) provide context. The within-class inverse calculation uses only the clique eigenspaces and closed walks on cycles. Priority for this corollary has not been established. The direct adjacency calculation in [strip_spectrum.py](strip_spectrum.py) compares every pair of seam types for the tested sizes.
