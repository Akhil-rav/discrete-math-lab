# Recovering parallel path lengths from one fixed number of colors

Let the simple graph have a core of r>=3 internally disjoint paths between the same two vertices, with positive lengths l_i and at most one direct edge. Trees may be attached at individual core or tree vertices; independent isolated vertices are irrelevant. From the distribution of X, the number of monochromatic edges for independent uniform q-coloring, one can recover the multiset of core path lengths for **every q>=3**. At q=2 the same holds for **odd r**, but fails in general at r=4. This is within-class reconstruction; it does not certify that an unknown graph has this form.

**Forward formula (standard).** Put m=max supp(X), x=qz/(1-z) and F(z)=E[(1+x)^X]/(1+x/q)^m. The maximum m is the number of edges because a constant coloring has positive probability. Attached tree edges divide out, by conditioning on their parent vertices and pruning leaves. For the core, conditioning on the two terminal colors and using the path transfer matrix gives

    q F(z) = product_i (1+(q-1)z^l_i) + (q-1) product_i (1-z^l_i).

This is the normalized two-terminal Potts formula; see [Sokal](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0012369) and the [three-path derivation](further-results.md). The experiment here fixes q and varies the edge weight; older chromatic uniqueness results vary the number of colors at proper-coloring weight, a different input. No priority assertion for the inverse statement.

**Inverse algorithm.** Let s=sum l_i. From F(1)=q^(r-1) recover r. The derivative of the second product vanishes at 1, and logarithmically differentiating the first gives F'(1)/F(1)=(q-1)s/q. Hence s too is available, even if the top coefficient cancels and the number of tree edges is unknown.

In the expansion of F, a subset of j paths contributes c_j z^(sum of their lengths), where c_j=((q-1)^j+(q-1)(-1)^j)/q. Read coefficients at exponents s-d for d=1,...,s. Each is a sum over *omitted* subsets of total length d. An omitted singleton contributes c_(r-1) times the multiplicity of length d. Every omitted subset of size at least two uses only lengths less than d, whose multiplicities were already reconstructed. Subtract those known contributions, counting labeled choices even at repeated lengths, and divide by c_(r-1). This coefficient is nonzero for all q>=3 and r>=3, and for q=2 and odd r. Induction proves recovery. The number of attached tree edges is m-s; their arrangement is not recovered.

**Binary obstruction.** For r=4 and q=2 the transform is just 1 plus the six pair-sum monomials plus z^s. Whenever replacing each length l by s/2-l produces admissible positive integer lengths, each new pair sum is the sum of the complementary old pair, giving the same transform. The distinct path multisets (2,2,2,4) and (1,3,3,3) both have s=10 and pair sums 4,4,4,6,6,6. The graphs are nonisomorphic: only the latter has adjacent degree-four terminals. With three colors the laws differ. This is the ordinary zero-field edge-count law; no claim about magnetic fields, general spin models or DP-colorings follows.

**Check.** The companion code verifies reconstruction on bounded multisets, tests the obstruction by direct vertex coloring, and checks that adding tree edges does not affect the recovered core. The proof, not the enumeration, covers arbitrary path lengths.

**Complete ambiguity at four paths.** Write the four lengths in order a<=b<=c<=d. The six pair sums have smallest two a+b,a+c, largest two b+d,c+d, and middle pair a+d,b+c (ties allowed). Fix which middle sum is b+c. Then a=((a+b)+(a+c)-(b+c))/2 determines b and c, and s determines d. Interchanging the middle choices yields the sorted tuple (s/2-d,s/2-c,s/2-b,s/2-a). Thus the above reflection and permutations exhaust the ambiguity for r=4. The invalid reflected tuples (nonintegral, nonpositive, or nonsimple) are discarded. The recovery of r and s works with attached trees as before.

**A partial positive binary result for even r.** Suppose r>=6 is not a power of two and the path lengths satisfy max(l_i)<2 min(l_i). The first choose(r,2) positive exponents in F, **counted with coefficient multiplicity**, are all the pair sums: any sum of four or more lengths is strictly larger than every pair sum. Let U_k be the k-th power sum of these pair sums and P_k the k-th power sum of the unknown lengths. Expanding over ordered pairs and removing the diagonal gives

    U_k = (r-2^(k-1))P_k + (1/2) sum_{i=1}^{k-1} choose(k,i) P_i P_(k-i).

Since the first coefficient never vanishes, recover P_1 through P_r recursively and hence the multiset by Newton identities. This is uniqueness **within the stated spread class**, not a proof ruling out an arbitrary competitor outside it. Pair-sum recovery itself is classical [Selfridge and Straus, On the determination of numbers by their sums of a fixed order](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/selfridgeorig.pdf). An exact search without a spread constraint through total length 70 found no collisions for r=6,8,10; respectively 295,962, 720,724 and 923,931 admissible multisets. The negative searches are not proofs. The r=4 search first collides at s=10. See theta_collisions.py.
