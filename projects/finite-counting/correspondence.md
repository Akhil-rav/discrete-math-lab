# When are random edge-conflict indicators independent?

Let each vertex of a finite simple graph choose an independent uniform color in [q], q>=2. Give each oriented edge uv a permutation pi of [q]; reversing the edge replaces pi by its inverse. Its indicator is 1 when color(v)=pi(color(u)). This models a **fixed full correspondence assignment**, not the minimum over covers defining the DP color function. We ask which families of these random variables are mutually independent, not which assignments are proper colorings.

**Criterion.** The indicators on an edge set S are mutually independent if and only if each component of S is a tree or has exactly one cycle and the permutation around that cycle has exactly one fixed color. The fixed-point count is unchanged by inversion or by changing the basepoint (conjugation).

**Proof.** In a connected component choose a spanning tree and a root. Once the root color is specified, imposing indicator one on every tree edge propagates the colors uniquely. Each remaining edge adds the requirement that a certain cycle permutation fix the root color. For v vertices and e edges the all-ones probability is therefore an integer divided by q^v, whereas independence would give q^(-e). If e>v no integer works, so two cycles in a component are impossible. If e=v the integer must be one, precisely the stated cycle condition. For the converse apply the same argument to *every subset*: subtrees have all q root colors available, and a surviving unique cycle has one. Distinct components use independent vertex colors. Thus every subset's all-ones probability factors; inclusion-exclusion gives every zero/one pattern. This is a direct probability consequence of classical gain-graph counting; compare [Zaslavsky's holonomy approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609048).

**Which palette sizes always give a matroid of independent indicators? Exactly q=2 and q=3.**

- At q=2 a permutation fixes either zero or two colors, never one. The criterion is precisely the ordinary graphic matroid.
- At q=3 a permutation has exactly one fixed color iff it is odd. Thus the admissible sets are forests and components with one negative cycle (edge signs are permutation signs), the signed-graphic frame matroid. An alternative verification: label colors by the field of order three; its six affine bijections exhaust all six permutations. For affine constraints over *any* finite field, consider the linear forms X_v-a_e X_u. For k selected forms of rank r, their simultaneous prescribed values have probability zero or q^(-r), equaling q^(-k) only if r=k. Full row rank makes the forms jointly uniform, hence the event indicators independent. Thus translations do not affect the independence matroid in the affine submodel. This is linear algebra; it does not identify the full joint distribution from the matroid.
- For every q>=4 use three internally disjoint length-two paths between two terminals. Along the three paths realize the respective total permutations id, A, B (put the nontrivial label on one edge), where A=(1 2), P=(1 2 ... q-1), B=A composed with P (rightmost acts first). A fixes q-2 colors; B fixes 1 and q and no others. Therefore each of the cycles pairing the identity path with A or B is minimally dependent: its cycle permutation does not have exactly one fixed point, whereas all proper edge subsets are forests. But A inverse composed with B is P, which fixes exactly one color. Remove a common edge of the identity path from the union of the two dependent cycles. What remains is just the independent A/B cycle with one pendant edge. This contradicts matroid circuit elimination. It is a counterexample on the same five-vertex graph K(2,3) for every q>=4.

**General group-action version.** Fix an allowed permutation subgroup H and let B={g in H: g has a number of fixed colors different from one}. The probability-independent sets form a matroid for every H-labeled graph **iff B is a subgroup**. This generalizes the palette-size calculation and specifies its scope.

Necessity: B contains the identity and is inverse-closed; if it is not a subgroup there exist u,v in B with u inverse times v outside B. Use total path labels id,u,v in the same circuit-elimination example. Sufficiency: a subgroup B here is normal since fixed-point counts are conjugacy-invariant. Pass to H/B. Cycles whose product is identity are exactly the disallowed cycles of a unicyclic component. The independent sets are therefore those of the frame matroid of this quotient gain graph (the established frame-matroid construction). For a direct exchange check, take admissible sets I,J and suppose no edge of J minus I can be added to I. Include isolated vertices as tree components. Every J edge incident to a tree component T of I must stay in T, since an edge joining it to any other component would be addable. Every internal J edge agrees with the vertex potentials induced by the gains along the tree, or its fundamental cycle would be nonidentity and it too would be addable. Hence every J cycle in T would have identity product and is forbidden; J has at most |T|-1 edges there. On the remaining vertices I has one edge per vertex in total, while J has at most that many. Thus |J|<=|I|, proving exchange.

**Distinctions from prior work.** [Signed graph representations](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(82)90033-6) and the balanced-cycle theta criterion in [biased / frame matroids](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2018.02.022) are established tools, not inventions here. Zaslavsky asks, among other things, when the **number of totally frustrated states** is a matroid invariant. Our question is whether *joint independence of edge events*, with the assignment fixed, defines matroid independent sets. These questions are not identical. For context on the special role of permutations fixing one color, see [Bailey, Cameron, Giudici and Royle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01950). The family-level criterion above is presented as a derivation, not a verified priority claim.

**A first mentorship exercise.** On a triangle with q=3 and one edge labeled by a transposition (others identity), each indicator has probability 1/3 and all three really are jointly independent. With q=4 a transposition fixes two colors and they are not. Enumerate all assignments before attempting the theta obstruction. This is closer to the subjects of [Halberg, Kaul, Liu, Mudrock, Shin and Thomason](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12897) than an unrelated general request for mentorship, while keeping their DP-color function distinct from this model.
