# Twelve weeks of deeper combinatorics work

**Planning assumption:** about five focused hours per week. This is a suggested
pace, not a promise of papers, awards, admission, or original results. Shorten the
scope if needed; keep the explanations and checks. No deadlines are scheduled.

| Week | Mathematical task | Deliverable | Understanding checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hall's condition and augmenting paths | Two-page explanation with one positive and one negative graph | Trace an augmenting path and exhibit a Hall obstruction without running code |
| 2 | Color partitions and rainbow matchings | Annotate a K33 witness and list its six perfect matchings | Explain why color names can be removed but vertex labels initially cannot |
| 3 | Even cycles | Write the proof A(C2n)=2n−2 for n≥2 | Give both an upper bound and an explicit lower-bound coloring |
| 4 | Prefix pruning in the sparse solver | Trace a rejected prefix; count its completions by hand | Derive T(r,k)=kT(r−1,k)+T(r−1,k+1) |
| 5 | Cube extremizers | Draw and check a seven-color witness from the data | Explain how every one of its nine perfect matchings repeats a color |
| 6 | Graph symmetries | Derive one K33 orbit/stabilizer calculation | Explain why color-class sizes do not determine a symmetry class |
| 7 | Read one matching paper carefully | A source note with exact hypotheses and convention dictionary | State the theorem in your own notation and explain the color-count shift |
| 8 | Design a bounded extension | A written host list, edge limit, independent oracle, and stopping rule | Explain why the proposed search is complete in its declared domain |
| 9 | Execute and examine the extension | Raw data, witnesses, and a reproducible command | Explain any disagreement before interpreting it mathematically |
| 10 | Proof or counterexample attempt | One precise statement with proof attempt or a checked counterexample | Separate observation from proof and identify the first unsupported step |
| 11 | Revise the flagship report | Clear introduction, definitions, methods, results, limitations | Present it in ten minutes without reading generated prose |
| 12 | Seek mathematical feedback | A specific question and a short packet ready for a teacher/mentor | Explain what feedback is needed and what was personally understood |

The lab contains an elementary cycle proof to learn from; rewriting it from
memory is a learning exercise, not a novelty claim. For the later extension,
novelty must be investigated independently before making any originality claim.

## A repeatable weekly routine

Use roughly two hours for definitions and proofs, two for one carefully scoped
experiment, and one for writing and review. Keep a dated journal of what you
tried, what failed, what you can now explain, and the next unresolved step.
Distinguish personal reasoning from AI suggestions, copied source statements,
and machine output. A failed attempt can be valuable evidence of understanding.

Do not publish a new repository simply to increase a count. Split a track when
it has its own coherent question, documentation, checks, and audience. The current
flagship should become a strong artifact before expanding into unrelated topics.
